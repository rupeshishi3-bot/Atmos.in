Weather Facts

Did You Know?

The shortest recorded rainfall lasted just 1 minute in Unionville, Maryland, USA on July 4, 1956.

Raindrops can fall at speeds of up to 22 mph (35 km/h) depending on their size.

Snow Records

The largest snowflake ever recorded measured 15 inches (38 cm) across, found in Fort Keogh, Montana in 1887.

No two snowflakes are exactly alike - each has a unique crystalline structure formed as it falls.

Lightning Facts

Lightning can reach temperatures of 50,000°F (27,760°C) - hotter than the surface of the sun.

The longest lightning bolt ever recorded stretched 440 miles (709 km) across Brazil in 2018.

Weather Through History

Historical Events

1815
Year Without a Summer
The eruption of Mount Tambora in Indonesia caused global cooling, leading to snow in June and widespread crop failures across North America and Europe.
1888
Great Blizzard
The Great Blizzard of 1888 dumped 40-50 inches of snow on the Northeastern United States, with drifts up to 50 feet high. Over 400 people died.
1931
China Floods
The 1931 China floods were among the deadliest natural disasters of the 20th century, with estimates of 1-4 million deaths from flooding along the Yangtze, Huang He, and Hai He rivers.
1970
Bhola Cyclone
The Bhola Cyclone struck East Pakistan (now Bangladesh) and West Bengal, India, killing an estimated 300,000-500,000 people. It remains the deadliest tropical cyclone ever recorded.
2005
Hurricane Katrina
Hurricane Katrina caused catastrophic damage along the Gulf Coast, particularly New Orleans. With damages exceeding $125 billion and over 1,800 fatalities, it was one of the costliest hurricanes in US history.

Temperature Records

1913
Hottest Temperature
The highest natural temperature ever recorded on Earth was 56.7°C (134°F) in Furnace Creek Ranch, Death Valley, California, USA on July 10, 1913.
1983
Coldest Temperature
The lowest natural temperature ever recorded on Earth was -89.2°C (-128.6°F) at the Soviet Vostok Station in Antarctica on July 21, 1983.
2021
European Heatwave
Southern Europe experienced record-breaking temperatures in August 2021, with Sicily potentially recording 48.8°C (119.8°F), which would be the highest temperature ever recorded in Europe.
